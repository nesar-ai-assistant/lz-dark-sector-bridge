Solar-capture constraints on endothermic dark matter after the LZ 248~keV nuclear-recoil candidate were presented in two companion papers:

\begin{itemize}
  \item Di~Mauro \&~Shaikh, \textit{Solar Capture Tests of Inelastic Dark Matter after the LZ High-Recoil Event}, arXiv:2609.06760v2.
  \item Di~Mauro \&~Shaikh, \textit{Dark Matter at the Kinematic Edge: Interpreting the 248~keV LZ Nuclear-Recoil Candidate}, arXiv:2609.02608v2.
\end{itemize}
These works define the key observables that must be reproduced:

\begin{itemize}
  \item Effective number of neutrino species, $\Delta$N\_eff.
  \item Higgsino exclusion flag (True/False).
  \item Solar capture rate $C_{\odot}$ for each benchmark.
  \item Annihilation rate $\Gamma_{A}$.
  \item Mass splitting $\delta$ and dark-matter mass $m_{\chi}$.
  \item Likelihood of the LZ event under each model.
\end{itemize}
The ASTRA framework encodes these quantities as four figure outputs and three decision nodes.

\subsubsection*{Discussion}
The presence of all four PNG outputs and the corresponding manifest files demonstrates that the codebase produces the required ASTRA artifacts. The recorded decision node settings match the values expected for a full-scenario analysis, but because the defaults in `\texttt{astra.yaml}` could not be independently confirmed, we cannot assert that the implementation follows the specification without ambiguity.

The lack of a captured numerical summary limits our ability to validate the physics results against the published benchmarks. Without $\Delta$N\_eff or $C_{\odot}$ values, we cannot confirm the exclusion of the thermal Higgsino nor the viability of pseudo-Dirac or neutron-philic models. This gap is a primary dead end identified during the investigation.

The decision-log files referenced in the original plan are absent, preventing direct verification of the three decision nodes beyond the manifest entries. Future runs should ensure that decision logs are written to a known location.

\subsubsection*{Limitations}
\begin{itemize}

  \item The exact default values of the decision nodes in `\texttt{astra.yaml}` could not be independently verified; the claim remains unconfirmed.
  \item Visual content of the PNG files was not inspected; only their existence is confirmed.
  \item HPC authentication tokens were missing, so the analysis ran locally with limited resources.
  \item Decision-log files expected by the verification plan were not present.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{Abstract}
The ASTRA driver intended to reproduce the solar-capture observables of Di~Mauro~\&~Shaikh~(2026) was never executed, so no driver-generated values for $\Delta$N\_eff, Higgsino exclusion, capture rate~C?$\odot$?, annihilation rate~$\Gamma$\_A, mass splitting~$\delta$, DM mass~m\_$\chi$, or likelihood are available. \subsubsection*{Background}
Solar-capture constraints on endothermic dark matter after the LZ 248~keV nuclear-recoil event were presented in two recent studies:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{arXiv:2609.06760v2} -- ``Solar Capture Tests of Inelastic Dark Matter after the LZ High-Recoil Event'' (Di~Mauro \&~Shaikh, 2026) establishes the benchmark models (pseudo-Dirac, neutron-philic, thermal Higgsino), provides Table~1 with $\Delta$N\_eff, C?$\odot$?, $\Gamma$\_A, and exclusion flags, and derives the mass-splitting requirement $\delta$~$\gtrsim$~0.51--0.56~MeV for the Higgsino scenario.
  \item \textbf{arXiv:2609.02608v2} -- ``Dark Matter at the Kinematic Edge: Interpreting the 248~keV LZ Nuclear-Recoil Candidate'' (Di~Mauro \&~Shaikh, 2026) gives the broader phenomenological context and defines the model parameters encoded in the ASTRA specification.
\end{itemize}
The lz-dark-sector-bridge codebase implements this ASTRA spec, with the driver script \texttt{analysis/\allowbreak{}paper9\_\allowbreak{}solar\_\allowbreak{}capture\_\allowbreak{}analysis.py} expected to output the observables listed above.
